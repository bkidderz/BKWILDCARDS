# ============================================================
# AUTUMNAL OXIDATION ACCENT PALETTE - KREA2
# Anti-monochrome accent wildcard - GOTHIC AUTUMN / OXIDATION variant.
# Each line forces at least TWO contrasting accent hues placed on wardrobe /
# lighting / environment (NOT skin), so a dominant color has something to
# contrast against instead of flooding the frame.
# Uses genre-native sources ONLY: rust and verdigris patina, dead-leaf and
# ember autumn foliage, tarnished gilt and bronze, oxblood and burgundy,
# candle and overcast light, cold fog. NO neon, NO signage. Inject alongside
# the outfit, or pin one line into the base prompt.
# ============================================================

# Rust / Patina
rust-orange ironwork against verdigris-green copper patina
corroded-iron orange against cold slate-grey stone
oxidized-copper teal against warm rust-brown accents
tarnished-bronze green against deep oxblood-red

# Foliage
dead-leaf amber against cold moss-green accents
burnt-orange foliage against a cool slate-blue sky
warm russet and ember-gold against deep charcoal shadow
faded ochre leaves against wet blackened bark

# Warm vs Cool
warm candle-gold against cold grey fog-light, a split-lit scene
guttering ember warmth against cold blue overcast daylight
lantern-amber highlights against deep teal shadow
a warm-lit figure against a cold misted environment

# Jewel
tarnished gold-gilt against deep-burgundy velvet accents
antique-gold and cold amethyst-violet accents
oxblood-red against cool tarnished-silver highlights
deep-garnet and verdigris-green jewel accents

# Metallic
wrought-iron black and warm rust accents against pale stone
tarnished brass fittings against cold grey slate
weathered-copper verdigris against warm mahogany brown

# Subject vs Environment
a warm-toned subject set against a distinctly cool-toned environment
a cool-toned subject set against a distinctly warm-toned environment
a saturated subject against a muted, desaturated background
the wardrobe in hues that deliberately contrast the subject's own coloring
